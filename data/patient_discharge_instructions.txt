PATIENT DISCHARGE INSTRUCTIONS
MediCare Health System — Inpatient Services
Version: 4.2 | Effective Date: February 2024

INTRODUCTION
These discharge instructions are provided to ensure a safe transition from hospital to home. Please read all sections carefully. Contact MediCare's 24-hour helpline at 1800-MED-CARE if you have any questions after discharge.

BEFORE YOU LEAVE THE HOSPITAL
1. Ensure you have received all prescribed medications or pharmacy prescriptions.
2. Collect all personal belongings including valuables deposited with the hospital.
3. Settle all outstanding bills at the billing desk or through the patient portal.
4. Confirm your follow-up appointment date with the ward nurse.
5. Ensure a responsible adult accompanies you home — patients who received anesthesia or sedation must not drive for 24 hours.
6. Collect a copy of your discharge summary — this is your official medical record of this hospitalization.

WOUND CARE
If you have a surgical wound or incision:
- Keep the wound clean and dry for the first 48 hours.
- Change dressings as instructed by your nurse or surgeon.
- Do not submerge the wound in water (no baths or swimming) until cleared by your physician.
- Watch for signs of infection: increased redness, swelling, warmth, discharge, or fever above 38.5°C (101.3°F).
- Do not apply lotions, creams, or any product to the wound unless prescribed.

MEDICATIONS AFTER DISCHARGE
- Take all prescribed medications as directed. Do not skip doses.
- Do not stop antibiotics before completing the full course even if you feel better.
- If you experience side effects, call your physician before stopping any medication.
- Do not take non-prescribed medications, supplements, or herbal products without consulting your physician.
- Pain medications: take only as prescribed. Do not exceed recommended dosage.

DIET AND NUTRITION
General guidelines post-hospitalization:
- Stay well hydrated: drink 8–10 glasses of water daily unless your physician has recommended fluid restriction.
- Follow any specific dietary instructions provided by your doctor or dietitian (e.g., low-sodium, low-fat, diabetic diet).
- Avoid alcohol for at least 48 hours after any procedure or while on antibiotics or pain medications.
- Small, frequent meals are recommended if you experience nausea or digestive discomfort.

ACTIVITY AND REST
- Rest is important for recovery. Avoid strenuous activity for the period specified by your doctor.
- Light walking is encouraged unless instructed otherwise.
- Do not lift objects heavier than 2 kg (4.4 lbs) for at least 2 weeks after abdominal surgery.
- Do not drive or operate heavy machinery for 24 hours after sedation or anesthesia, or while on opioid pain medications.
- Sexual activity should be resumed only after physician clearance.

FOLLOW-UP APPOINTMENTS
- Your follow-up appointment has been scheduled. Refer to your discharge summary for date, time, and location.
- If you need to reschedule, contact the outpatient appointment desk at least 48 hours in advance.
- Missing follow-up appointments may delay your recovery. Please attend all scheduled visits.
- Bring your discharge summary and a list of current medications to every follow-up appointment.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE
Go to the nearest emergency room or call 112 immediately if you experience:
- Chest pain or pressure, or difficulty breathing.
- Sudden confusion, slurred speech, or inability to move a limb.
- Uncontrolled bleeding that does not stop with direct pressure.
- High fever above 39°C (102.2°F) that does not respond to medication.
- Severe abdominal pain.
- Loss of consciousness.
- Signs of deep vein thrombosis: sudden calf pain, redness, or swelling in the leg.

MENTAL HEALTH SUPPORT
It is normal to feel anxious, emotional, or overwhelmed after a hospital stay:
- Talk to a trusted family member or friend about your feelings.
- MediCare's mental health helpline is available at 1800-MED-MIND (Mon–Fri, 8 AM–8 PM).
- Telehealth mental health consultations can be booked through the patient portal.

CAREGIVER INSTRUCTIONS
If a family member or caregiver is assisting with recovery:
- Medication reminders: help the patient follow medication schedules.
- Monitor vital signs if equipment (BP monitor, glucometer) has been provided.
- Watch for any warning signs listed in the emergency section above.
- Ensure patient does not engage in prohibited activities during recovery.
